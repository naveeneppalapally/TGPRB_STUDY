headline: BSIP Pollen Study at Deoria Tal Links Harappan Contraction to '4.2 ka' Abrupt Climate Event
exam_fact: Analyzing a sediment core from Deoria Tal in Garhwal Himalaya, Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences (BSIP) scientists traced the shrinking of Harappan settlements along the Indus and Ghaggar-Hakra rivers to an abrupt cooling/dryness event at 4,200 cal yr BP ('4.2 ka event').
summary: Scientists from the Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences (BSIP, DST), led by Dr. Mohammad Firoze Quamar, studied an AMS radiocarbon-dated pollen sediment core from Deoria Tal in the Garhwal Himalaya. The record pinned an abrupt dry-cooling phase around 4,200 cal yr BP (the global '4.2 ka event' marking the Meghalayan Age), driven by a southward ITCZ shift, strong El Nino and negative Indian Ocean Dipole, explaining Harappan migration from the Indus and Ghaggar-Hakra basins toward the east Ganga plains.
